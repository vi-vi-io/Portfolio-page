BeFi Conversations - Digital Fiduciaries (05:50)

BeFi Conversations was Voya Financial's thought leadership series for current and prospective large and mega market retirement plan clients. Each episode paired Voya leadership with experts from the Voya Behavioral Finance Institute for Innovation, to show how behavioral science can improve retirement outcomes, and to position Voya as a thought leader among retirement recordkeepers. It was self-promotion that doubled as education.

In “Digital Fiduciaries,” Charlie Nelson, Voya's CEO of Retirement and Employee Benefits, sits down with UCLA professor Shlomo Benartzi to discuss how digital design shapes retirement saving decisions, and why plan sponsors and advisors may need to act as digital fiduciaries.

The challenge

The audience was senior benefits decision-makers with little time to spare. The content was an unscripted conversation between an executive and an academic. The series had to look polished enough to carry the Voya brand, while keeping the energy of a real conversation.

One shoot, several episodes

We filmed more than one episode in a single shoot, so the camera, lighting and sound setup had to work for a series rather than a single video. I returned in the same roles for a follow-up shoot, for “Using Behavioral Economics to Help Improve Financial Wellbeing,” in which Charlie Nelson, Rick Mason and Shlomo Benartzi cover new plan designs, a better employer match built on behavioral principles, new ways of framing decisions, and advances in personalization.

The series look

We shot at Voya's corporate headquarters in the Helmsley Building, with the view down Park Avenue behind the conversation. I ran camera and lighting, and had a voice in the direction on set, with three Panasonic GH5s: two locked-off angles, and a third on a Rhino slider to add movement. Shooting in 4K for an HD deliverable gave me room to reframe in the edit, for punch-ins and cutaways without adding cameras.

Sound

Each speaker was recorded on two microphones, a lavalier and a boom, for four channels in all. That gave the mix redundancy and the cleanest blend for each voice.

Post-production

Most of my work came in post. I edited the episode, shaping a long conversation into a focused piece that moves between three angles, and designed and animated its graphics in After Effects to keep the key ideas on screen.

The result

Years after release, “Digital Fiduciaries” is still featured on Voya's Behavioral Finance page for plan sponsors and advisors.

Podcast adaptation

I later re-cut “Digital Fiduciaries” as an audio-only podcast episode. It has a new cold open, a host open and close, and an interview edit restructured for listening, so the points the graphics carried on screen still land by ear.
